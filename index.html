    What the new fee is

    A small fee on bigger shop payments.

    From 15 Oct 2026, a fee called MDR (merchant discount rate) applies to UPI payments to shops above ₹2,000: 0.4% of the amount, never more than ₹300.

    The fee curve

    Zero, then 0.4%, then a ceiling.

    Nothing up to ₹2,000. Then 0.4% of the amount, which stops rising at ₹300 once a payment reaches ₹75,000. Fuel, insurance, utilities, telecom and railways pay a flat ₹5. Mutual funds and stocks pay 0.02%.

    "Free" was never free

    An ₹800 UPI payment costs about ₹2 to process.

    The RBI broke down who carries that cost. NPCI, which runs UPI, bears only about 8%. Banks and apps carry the rest.

    ₹0.80Your bank40%

    Source: RBI Discussion Paper on Charges in Payment Systems, 17 Aug 2022, Box 9 (approximate costs)

    Who has been paying

    Your taxes covered part of the bill.

    Since 2020 the government has paid banks and apps an incentive to keep UPI free. It is a fraction of what the system costs to run each year, which is why the fee is arriving.

    How we got here

    A decade in six dates

    1. 2016

      UPI launches.

    2. 1 Jan 2020

      Zero-fee rule begins. Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act bars charges on UPI.

    3. 17 Aug 2022

      RBI publishes a discussion paper on payment charges, including what UPI really costs to run.

    4. 8 Aug 2026

      Finance Ministry explains the law change that lets NPCI's steering committee set a fee on merchant payments.

    5. 15 Sep 2026

      NPCI publishes the rules: 0.4% on shop payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300.

    6. 15 Oct 2026

      The merchant fee takes effect.

    The verdict

    The short version: not much to worry about.

    • Payments of ₹2,000 or less carry no fee. That's more than 95 in 100.
    • Sending money to people stays free, at any amount.
    • Above ₹2,000, the fee is 0.4%, never more than ₹300. Fuel, bills and insurance: a flat ₹5.
